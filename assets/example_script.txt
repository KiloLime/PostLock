Your phone battery is lying to you, and it's been doing it for years.
That "20 percent" warning is not a prediction, it's a marketing decision.
Battery makers could give you a real gauge, but most phones just estimate.
They use a simple voltage guess, and voltage drops are anything but linear.
At the very end, the curve falls off a cliff — one minute it says 5%, the next it's dead.
So the phone picks a safe number, 20%, and screams at you to charge.
That's why the last 20% seems to vanish faster than the first 80%.
Some phones even hide a tiny reserve below zero to save your data from the cutoff.
Your battery isn't dying faster, it just can't see the future.
Next time it hits 20%, you're really at 24% — and now you know the trick.
